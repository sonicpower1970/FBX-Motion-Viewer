# v0.2.8 — Viewer / MP4 composition

## Cause identified in v0.2.7

With FIT OFF and FOLLOW ON, export evaluated Animation START and reset the Follow reference, but retained the camera world position and Orbit target from the inspected (possibly middle/end) frame. The subject moved back to START while the camera did not. The resulting subject-relative position was wrong by the root displacement. Merely resetting Follow history cannot correct that initial offset. Rotation, FOV and zoom were not the cause of this root-motion mismatch.

The prior centered perspective crop adjusted FOV/aspect. Unit projection comparisons confirm that calculation was valid for ordinary symmetric perspective cameras; changing aspect alone would not have been valid. Reconstructing a projection from lens parameters is nevertheless less general than cropping the actual matrix and can lose consistency with off-axis/view-offset projection. v0.2.8 removes that reconstruction. No empirical camera-distance correction is used.

## Export initialization

1. Snapshot time/play state, camera (including quaternion, FOV, zoom, projection/inverse), Orbit target, FIT/FOLLOW and Follow internal reference. Capture the inspected subject world position before seeking.
2. Use an independent camera clone and independent Follow controller. The live camera is never fitted or followed during encoding.
3. Evaluate START exactly. FIT ON uses the existing guide-aware Fit on the clone with the original Viewer aspect. FIT OFF/FOLLOW OFF leaves camera and Orbit target unchanged. FIT OFF/FOLLOW ON translates both by `subjectStart - subjectInspected`, maintaining their relative positions, orientation and lens.
4. Initialize Follow at START; apply output projection crop after Fit. Evaluate each output timestamp explicitly and apply ordinary ground Follow on the export camera.
5. Save the MP4, restoring the Viewer snapshot on success, failure or cancellation as before. The live scene/renderer are still reused during export; camera and Follow history are independent.

The initial subject-relative relocation includes XYZ to preserve the inspected relative composition even if the inspected pose is airborne. Subsequent Follow is still ground-only (XZ); jumps are not followed vertically. Loop policy is unchanged from v0.2.7.

## Projection

For Viewer dimensions W×H and its centered contained output gate w×h, the export matrix is `diag(W/w, H/h, 1, 1) × viewerProjection`. This maps exactly the Guide interior to the full output frame, including asymmetric perspective, zoom, film offset and view offsets. The inverse matrix is recomputed. Camera FOV/zoom fields retain their original values; the matrix carries the gate crop. Do not call `updateProjectionMatrix()` after cropping.

720p/1080p use the same 16:9 gate whether the Guide overlay is shown or hidden. Current Viewport keeps the full original frustum; existing even-pixel encoder rounding is retained. Fit is performed before cropping so its guide-aware bounds and angle match a Viewer START Fit.

Burn-in still uses the shared renderer: Viewer labels are relative to the Guide rectangle, movie labels to the full output rectangle. The Guide itself never enters the MP4.

## Compatibility and manual checks

No new dependencies or OS-specific APIs. Legacy parser/WASM, texture policy, local-only handling, offline frame stepping, save-picker/fallback, and export resolutions remain unchanged. Chrome/WebCodecs capability remains required for MP4. Windows/Linux physical devices are not tested here.

On production root-motion material, compare START/middle/near-END export starts with FIT OFF/FOLLOW ON at side/low/high angles and zoom. For each comparison, keep the same subject-relative composition. Compare Guide interior against 720p/1080p, including long filenames. Verify fixed-camera FIT OFF/FOLLOW OFF, START Fit when ON, Current Viewport, and restoration after completion/cancel/save failure. FIT ON intentionally replaces manual framing at START; different animated poses may occupy different screen areas even with identical camera-relative root framing.
